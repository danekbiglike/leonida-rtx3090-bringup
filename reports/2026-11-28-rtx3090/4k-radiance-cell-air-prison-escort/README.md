# RTX 3090 prison escort with radiance-cell volumetric lighting

Lucia followed the officer from A-102 into the dayroom. At Series X settings and 3840 × 2160 output the game presented **2,026 frames in 120 seconds (16.88 FPS)**. Five-second averages ranged from 13.4 to 18.4 FPS. The 95th-percentile interval was 82.19 ms and the longest was 129.18 ms. The previous depth-audit run on this route averaged 17.62 FPS, with a 12.4 FPS minimum, a 75.93 ms 95th percentile and a 219.09 ms maximum. The two runs also differ in the resource retirement queues and CPU instrumentation. The shorter maximum in this sample cannot be attributed to the shader. The guard and Lucia were visible at A-102. Figures near the camera still appeared as dark silhouettes in the dayroom. After the cell timing window we captured 13 additional angles. The four strongest orange-grid candidates were the cabinet and containers, the lit wall, and the view through the wire window. None reproduced the original paired orange rings. A matching image of those rings is still needed to establish whether the shader fixes them. The separate two-minute interval starting in the cell produced 1,449 presents (12.08 FPS), 6.4 FPS in the worst five seconds and a 2.009 s maximum interval. During the escort Windows reported at least 4.54 GiB of physical memory available. For the 2,007 presents covered by complete CPU sampling intervals the submission thread used 39.16 ms of CPU per present. The six recording threads used 137.92 ms combined. The renderer compiled the new volumetric lighting shader with the original 160 × 90 directional-light grid. It retained full GPU write declarations, with the read-only audit and slow mapped-read timers disabled. Hardware was a Ryzen 7 3700X and RTX 3090, using SSD storage, without DLSS or frame generation.
